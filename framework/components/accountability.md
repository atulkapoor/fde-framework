Being able to say why a particular decision came out the way it did, to somebody
outside the team, months later.

Separate from governance on purpose. A boundary answers *where data may go*;
this answers *why this outcome*. They are triggered by different things and a
system frequently needs both -- a regulated lender has a residency constraint
and an obligation to explain a refusal, and folding them into one component
means whichever fires first hides the other.

Built in from the start or not at all. Reconstructing why a model decided
something after the fact is guesswork wearing a report's formatting, and the
question always arrives when the original context is gone.
