Neither cleared the 85% bar, so the bounded loop refused both; 72.6% stands as the measured ceiling of a 0.6B extractor on noisy OCR. Throughput: ~102 tok/s on 17GB Apple-silicon unified memory.

Judging — the calibration gate, run for real

The third demonstration (freeform Q&A over real documents) puts the same 0.6B model through the framework's judge-calibration gate: hand-graded outputs against the model's verdicts, agreement measured before any judged number is trusted. The published record (SLMJury) measured this model at 67.9% agreement — below any honest bar. Our run either replicates that first-party or surprises us; whichever happens is the finding, and it lands here with a link to the reproducible run.

From the published record

Extraction (schema-bound): a sub-1B model may honestly hold this job — the fixed-schema task is far easier than the instruction-following benchmarks suggest (qwen3-0.6b: IFEval 54.5 [Qwen3 TR], yet 72.6% measured above). Specialist extractors are the staged upgrade: the LFM2-Extract family (1.2B) reports ~99.6% JSON validity and parity with 27B-class generalists on document extraction [model card] — vendor-run numbers, so clear your own gate first.

Judging (reference-based) has a measured floor. SLMJury [arXiv 2606.07810] tested judges 0.6B–14B on 64k reference-based verdicts: qwen3-0.6b agrees with human graders 67.9% of the time; qwen3-4b, 87.6%; phi-4-mini, 88.2%; qwen3-8b, 89.0% — the curve saturates near 4B. Below 4B-class, a judge adds noise faster than signal.

Verdicts, never scores. Small judges reach ~ρ0.57 on open-ended 0–1 scoring versus near-88% agreement on discrete verdicts — which is why the framework's emitted judge asks correct / partial / incorrect and maps to {1, 0.5, 0}. And the reference answer in the prompt is what makes a small judge legitimate at all; without one, none of this size class is.

What the framework does with this

  • fde scan <engagement> names the runtime, the models sized to the measured hardware, and the export line — dated, and concurrency-aware past ~1000 requests/day.
  • fde next knows the wall: a build that calls a model with no endpoint standing gets "run fde scan" as its next move, not silence.
  • The judge is never trusted before calibration against a human grader — an uncalibrated judge is a random number generator with a monthly bill.
